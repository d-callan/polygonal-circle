# Universality and Waveform Invariance in Recursive Polygonal Circles

## Abstract

We demonstrate that the **recursive polygonal circle model exhibits strict universality**: its critical point, phase transitions, critical exponents, and asymptotic fractal dimensions are **independent of the microscopic tooth waveform $\phi(u)$**. 

Whether the polygonal ripples are generated by:
1. Piecewise linear triangle waves ($C^0$),
2. Smooth pure sinusoidal harmonics ($C^\infty$),
3. Piecewise quadratic parabolic curves ($C^1$),
4. Asymmetric multi-frequency harmonic sums ($C^\infty$), or
5. Steep hyperbolic tangent square waves ($C^\infty$),

the macroscopic scaling behavior is governed by the same **universal Infrared (IR) and Ultraviolet (UV) fixed points**. All microscopic waveforms collapse onto the identical universal master curve.

## 2. Four Universal Invariants

### Invariant 1: The Critical Threshold $a_c = 1/b$
The critical shrinkage factor $a_c$ where perimeters transition from finite to divergent is **identically $1/b$** across all five waveforms:
$$\forall \phi \in \{\text{Triangle}, \text{Sine}, \text{Parabolic}, \text{Multi-Harmonic}, \text{Square}\}, \quad a_c = \frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333$$

### Invariant 2: The Smooth Euclidean Circle as Universal Infrared Fixed Point
When $a < a_c$ (the convergent regime, e.g. $a = 0.20$):
- All high-frequency tooth oscillations contract geometrically: $\lim_{n \to \infty} a^n = 0$.
- For every waveform, the perimeter converges to a finite rectifiable limit.
- The box-counting and Richardson coastline dimensions converge identically to **$D = 1.0000$**.
- The smooth Euclidean circle is the **universal Infrared (IR) attractor** for any periodic microscopic generator.

### Invariant 3: The Linear Critical Exponent $\beta = 1$
Near the critical point, the order parameter $\Psi(a) = D(a) - 1$ turns on with a linear critical exponent:
$$\Psi(a) \propto (a - a_c)^\beta, \qquad \beta = 1 \quad (\text{Universal})$$
The derivative jump at $a_c$ is universally:
$$\Delta\left(\frac{d\Psi}{da}\right) = \frac{b}{\ln b} \approx 2.7307$$

### Invariant 4: The Universal Asymptotic Fractal Dimension
In the supercritical regime ($a > 1/b$, e.g. $a = 0.50$):
- Regardless of the waveform, the asymptotic perimeter growth ratio satisfies:
  $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{P_{n+1}}{P_n} = b \cdot a = 1.5000$$
- The fractal dimension is uniquely determined by $b$ and $a$:
  $$D = 1 + \frac{\ln(ba)}{\ln b} = 1 + \frac{\ln(1.5)}{\ln(3)} \approx 1.3691$$
